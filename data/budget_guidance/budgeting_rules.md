# Budgeting rules of thumb

## The 50/30/20 rule
A simple starting split of take-home income: about 50% for needs (rent, utilities, groceries, transport to work, insurance, minimum loan payments), 30% for wants (eating out, shopping, entertainment, subscriptions), and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It is a guideline, not a law: in expensive cities needs often take more than 50%, and the useful move is to shrink wants rather than abandon saving.

## Pay yourself first
Move the savings amount out of the spending account as soon as income arrives, instead of saving whatever is left at month end. An automatic monthly transfer to a savings account, recurring deposit or SIP removes the daily decision and makes saving the default.

## Needs versus wants
A quick test: if skipping it for a month would cause real harm (no home, no food, no way to work, no cover), it is a need. Most overspending is found in wants that grew gradually: food delivery, impulse shopping, and subscriptions nobody uses.

## Track categories monthly
Compare each category with the previous month. A category that rises two months in a row deserves attention even if the amount looks small. Large one-off purchases should be judged separately from habits that repeat every week.

## Set a cap per category
Choose a monthly limit for the two or three categories that grew most, and review progress mid-month. Caps work better when they are slightly tighter than the current spend instead of a drastic cut that is abandoned in a week.
