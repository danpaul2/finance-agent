# Practical saving tactics

## Small UPI payments add up
Many small payments of a few hundred rupees feel harmless individually but can become a large monthly total, and they are hard to remember because each one is tiny. Review the count of small payments as well as their total. Practical fixes: set a weekly cash-like budget for small purchases, turn on spending notifications, and ask before each small payment whether it was planned.

## Audit subscriptions
List every recurring charge: streaming, music, cloud storage, apps, memberships. Cancel anything unused for a month, share family plans where terms allow, and prefer annual plans only for services you are sure to keep. Check the statement for charges that repeat on the same day each month.

## Reduce food delivery
Food delivery carries delivery fees, platform fees and menu markups. Cutting the number of orders per week is usually more effective than hunting for coupons. Plan two or three home-cooked meals for busy days, and treat delivery as a deliberate choice rather than the default.

## Shopping: wait before buying
For non-essential purchases, wait 48 hours before paying. Remove saved cards from shopping apps, unsubscribe from sale alerts, and keep a wishlist instead of buying immediately. Many wants fade within a couple of days.

## Transport costs
Compare the monthly cost of frequent cab or auto rides with a pass, metro or bus, or sharing rides. For regular commutes, a fixed monthly option is often cheaper than paying per trip.

## Avoid bank charges
Keep a minimum balance if your account requires it, avoid cash withdrawals beyond the free ATM limit, and set reminders for card and loan due dates so no late fees apply. Bank charges give nothing in return, so they are the easiest costs to eliminate.
