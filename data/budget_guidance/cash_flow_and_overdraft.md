# Cash flow, balances and emergency funds

## Keep a buffer in the spending account
Keep at least one week to one month of expected expenses in the account used for daily payments. A balance that regularly drops close to zero or below zero causes stress, fees and failed payments. If the balance runs low in the same part of every month, the problem is timing or total spending, and both can be fixed with a plan.

## Overdraft and negative balance
A negative balance means spending exceeded available funds, often with interest and charges added by the bank. First stop non-essential payments, then move money back from savings only if needed, and rebuild the buffer. Review what pushed the balance down: a few large payments or many small ones.

## Emergency fund
Aim to build an emergency fund covering roughly three to six months of essential expenses, kept somewhere safe and easy to access, such as a savings account or liquid fund. Even a small starting target, such as one month of expenses, makes a difference.

## Savings are not spending
Transfers to your own savings, recurring deposits or investments are not consumption. They should be counted as saving, and the goal is to make them regular and automatic.

## Plan for irregular costs
Insurance premiums, annual fees and festival spending arrive in lumps. Divide the yearly amount by twelve and set that aside each month so a large bill does not cause an overdraft.
